# Field Context From Venezuelan Diaspora And Frontline Contacts

Date captured: 2026-06-26

This note records firsthand context shared by a Venezuelan diaspora contact with family, friends, and trusted frontline relationships in the affected area. It should be treated as operationally valuable local context, not as independently verified public reporting.

## Situation Snapshot

- Emergency response and rescue efforts are ongoing.
- Time is a critical constraint. Many hours have passed since the earthquake, and at least one frontline contact reported spending the day in one of the worst-affected areas where teams were mostly recovering bodies from rubble.
- Some family members and friends of the contact are safe, but the situation is severe for many others.

## Connectivity And Power

- La Guaira, described by the contact as part of the coastline near the epicentral area, is currently without power.
- Internet access is intermittent.
- Starlink has reportedly enabled some connectivity hotspots, but people do not have a clear, reliable map of where those hotspots are.
- The initial product should therefore prioritize mapping connectivity hotspots alongside relief locations and support centers.

## Communication Channels

Common channels in affected communities:

- WhatsApp;
- Instagram;
- Facebook;
- SMS;
- X.

Most people in the worst-affected areas may not have smartphones. Many people providing assistance on the ground do have smartphones and can act as proxy reporters, validators, or upload points for people without devices.

## Family Location And Tracing

Locating family members is a major challenge.

Observed behaviors:

- people write relatives' names on walls;
- people share missing/found information through WhatsApp, Instagram, and X;
- support centers, shelters, and informal support groups are difficult to locate.

Product implication: the first release should include a missing/found person workflow that supports proxy entry, wall-list transcription, screenshot OCR, duplicate matching, and privacy-preserving family contact updates.

## Local Relationship Network

The contact has friends and family members in the affected area who know people and organizations working on the front line.

Product implication: the project should start with a trusted-network model:

- identify a small group of frontline smartphone users;
- give them simple forms for connectivity hotspots, support centers, missing/found notices, and urgent needs;
- use them as validators for local landmarks and duplicate reports;
- avoid public release of sensitive person-level data.

## Immediate Product Priorities From This Context

1. **Connectivity and support map**
   Map Starlink or other connectivity hotspots, charging points, support centers, shelters, clinics, food/water points, and collection centers.

2. **Proxy reporting**
   Allow responders with smartphones to submit reports for people without smartphones or internet.

3. **Family tracing**
   Capture missing/found reports from wall lists, WhatsApp forwards, Instagram/X posts, shelter lists, and hospital sightings.

4. **Offline/low-bandwidth design**
   Assume no power, intermittent internet, shared devices, battery scarcity, and delayed sync.

5. **Public-safe information**
   Publish support locations and connectivity points when verified; keep exact missing-person and vulnerable-person data private.
